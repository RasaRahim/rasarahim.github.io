Profile

About

I am a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at the State Key Laboratory of Fire Science (SKLFS), University of Science and Technology of China (USTC). I received my Ph.D. in Safety Science and Engineering from USTC in 2025 under the supervision of Professor Weiguo Song, with interdisciplinary expertise spanning civil engineering, physics, computer science, mathematics, and psychology.

My research focuses on fire safety, human behavior in emergencies, crowd dynamics, evacuation modeling, and human-environment interactions. I combine computational modeling, experimental methods, and data analytics to support safety and risk management in complex environments such as high-rise buildings, transit hubs, and public spaces.

Education: Hefei University of Technology (civil engineering); University of Science and Technology of China (PhD, 2025). Recognition: Outstanding International Graduate of USTC (2025).

I am motivated by improving emergency preparedness, optimizing evacuation strategies, and strengthening urban resilience, in line with UN SDGs 3, 9 and 11. I welcome collaboration on safety engineering, human-centered design for emergencies, resilient infrastructure, and disaster risk reduction.

Research focus

Research at the intersection of people, environments & models

Established

Pedestrian & crowd dynamics

Empirical analysis of pedestrian trajectories, route preferences, congestion, multidirectional flow, and movement behavior.

Established

Staircase evacuation

Human–obstacle interactions, stairway geometry, speed fluctuations, and safety implications for high-rise buildings.

Modeling

Computational evacuation

Behavioral and agent-based approaches, including optimal-step and social-force modeling, for evaluating evacuation performance.

Active development

Cognitive evacuation

Investigating risk perception, uncertainty, attention, and behavioral adaptation during evolving emergencies.

Experimental methods

Immersive VR & sensing

Developing Unity-based evacuation experiments and exploring eye-tracking and multimodal measurements.

Computational methods

Vision & trajectory analysis

Video-based pedestrian tracking, trajectory reconstruction, simulation integration, and reproducible quantitative analysis.
